Before you begin

Five practical answers.

01

Who should participate

A bank or payment firm evaluating scam review and local transaction monitoring. Bring a compliance owner, an analyst or operations lead, and an IT contact. Agree the evaluation question together.

02

What your team supplies

An agreed local dataset and its permitted use, account and payment history, and the outcomes available for evaluation. Your team provides access to suitable infrastructure, including an NVIDIA GPU, and completes its own security, legal and procurement checks.

03

What ZQUAS supplies

The pilot software, support to configure scam review and local monitoring, the signed decision log and its verification check. We agree the setup, evaluation support and report with your team before the pilot starts.

04

What gets measured

Scam payments caught, at most 1 review per 500 payments and 1 hold per 1,000, analyst workload, local monitoring workload and coverage, every decision verifiable in the signed log. These are evaluation measures and limits, not promised detection rates. Local transaction monitoring selects investigation work and does not itself hold payments.

05

What happens afterwards

Review the evidence, limitations and operational fit together. Decide whether to continue locally, change the scope, or stop. An optional network phase requires a separate agreement on participants, sharing policy, security, legal basis and success measures.

The local phase

12 weeks, with a clear review at the end.

The local pilot starts after scope, agreements, access and data readiness are confirmed. The plan below is agreed with your team before the start.

  1. Weeks 1 to 2

    Agree the scope

    Confirm the evaluation question, local data, access, responsibilities and success measures.

  2. Weeks 3 to 4

    Configure the local pilot

    Prepare local inputs and configuration for scam review and transaction monitoring. Check the decision log and verification process.

  3. Weeks 5 to 8

    Run the local evaluation

    Evaluate scam review and local transaction monitoring. Review nominated work with analysts. Record outcomes, monitoring coverage and workload against the agreed limits.

  4. Weeks 9 to 10

    Assess the results

    Examine scam payments caught, adherence to review and hold limits, analyst workload, local monitoring workload and coverage, and signed-log verification.

  5. Weeks 11 to 12

    Make the next decision

    Document results and limitations. Decide whether to continue locally or scope an optional network phase.

Data sovereignty

Your records stay with you.

No customer records leave your infrastructure. With network sharing switched on, the bank sends only bounded answers about payments it is part of, each one authorized by its own policy and recorded in its signed log.

Network participation is optional and follows the local phase by separate agreement. Current federation security is semi-honest: it assumes participants follow the protocol.
